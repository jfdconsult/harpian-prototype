A·01Risk Budget — Core Concept
Risk Budget
Maximum risk client can take
= min(RN_appetite, RN_capacity)
The tighter constraint always wins.
Behavioral floor beats financial floor when lower.
Risk Used
Current portfolio risk
= RN_portfolio (current)
This is what the portfolio actually carries today.
Calculated from upload — not estimated.
Risk Available
Remaining capacity to deploy
= Risk Budget − Risk Used
Positive → can take more risk.
Negative → already overdrawn. Must reduce.

Risk Available = the "fuel" for the allocation engine. Every slider move consumes or releases fuel. When Risk Available → 0, sliders lock. When negative (overdrawn), the engine escalates to a hard warning — not a soft yellow label.

Critical Rule — Budget Governs All Decisions
Budget positive → engine may suggest adding allocation
Every unit of Risk Available maps to a specific additional % in C11 or C22
Engine never suggests allocation that would bring Risk Available below zero
Budget overdrawn → engine enters REDUCTION mode
No allocation suggestions shown — only deallocation guidance
Primary action changes from "Apply Allocation" to "Reduce Exposure"
A·02Decision Envelope — Zones, Not Points
The goal is NOT a number.
The goal is to stay inside the Optimal Band — a range defined by four zone boundaries.
A portfolio at RN 48 or RN 54 is equally aligned if both are inside the band. Precision obsession is a UX failure.

B·01Core 11 vs Core 22 — Explicit Product Roles
Core 11
Efficiency tool · Fine-tuning instrument
  • Volatility Low · ↓ reduces portfolio vol
  • Return contribution Moderate · steady
  • Drawdown impact ↓ significant reduction
  • RN effect ↓ dilutes high-RN portfolios
  • P(Goal) effect ↑ modest — via reduced risk
  • Correlation benefit High · low corr to LatAm mix
  • StormGuard Armor Active · crisis exits automatically
Use Core 11 when: portfolio is over-risked · behavioral alignment needed · drawdown must be reduced · client is conservative to moderate
VS
Core 22
Performance engine · Return accelerator
  • Volatility Higher · ↑ increases portfolio vol
  • Return contribution Strong · primary return driver
  • Drawdown impact ↑ moderate increase
  • RN effect ↑ increases blended RN
  • P(Goal) effect ↑ significant — via return lift
  • Correlation benefit Moderate · some corr with equity
  • Trend regime Active · strong in bull trends
Use Core 22 when: portfolio is under-risked · goal gap exists · return acceleration needed · budget has room · client is moderate to aggressive
Decision Rule — Which Product to Recommend First
IF RN_portfolio > RN_appetite:
    → Core 11 ONLY.  Core 22 is contraindicated. Adding C22 worsens the behavioral breach.

IF RN_portfolio < RN_requirement AND Risk_Available > 5:
    → Core 22 first (return gap), then Core 11 to smooth (if budget allows).

IF RN_portfolio within optimal band:
    → Core 11 for efficiency enhancement only. Core 22 optional if budget > 10 pts remaining.

IF RN_appetite < RN_requirement (structural conflict):
    → Neither product can resolve this. Show horizon/target revision options.

B·03Emotional Clarity — System Messages
Institutional tone. No exclamation marks. No marketing language.
Each message is generated from a condition — not from copy. One sentence. Always factual. Always linked to a number.
"Your portfolio is safer than your goal requires. You have room to add targeted exposure without exceeding your tolerance."
Trigger: RN_portfolio < RN_requirement − 8 AND Risk_Available > 5
"You are well positioned to reach your target. Maintain this structure or make minor efficiency adjustments."
Trigger: Optimal zone AND P_goal ≥ 75%
"You are taking more risk than your profile supports. A moderate rebalancing will bring your portfolio within tolerance."
Trigger: Limit zone — RN 5–10 above appetite
"Your current portfolio exceeds your risk tolerance by [N] points. Structural adjustment is required before making further allocations."
Trigger: Danger zone — RN > appetite + 10
"Your target requires a level of risk your profile cannot sustain. This cannot be resolved by allocation alone. Review your objective or time horizon."
Trigger: RN_appetite < RN_requirement (structural conflict)
"Adding Core 11 at the suggested level will reduce your expected drawdown by [N]% while maintaining a trajectory toward your stated goal."
Trigger: Over-risked AND C11 allocation resolves gap
Messages are never shown simultaneously. Priority: Danger > Structural conflict > Limit > Under-risk > Optimal. Exactly one message visible at all times. Never generic. Always includes a number.

C·03The Core UX Principle — Non-Negotiable

This is not a dashboard.
This is a decision engine.

A dashboard shows information. A decision engine eliminates ambiguity and produces a clear action.
The user must never wonder what to do next.
5 seconds Status understood.
Verdict visible.
No reading required.
15 seconds Recommendation absorbed.
Numbers reviewed.
Range understood.
30 seconds Action taken.
Allocation applied
or strategy adjusted.
Developer Mandates — Absolute, Non-Negotiable
Must Always Be True
· One verdict. One emotional message. One primary button.
· Zone color fills entire screen context — not just a badge
· Risk Budget widget visible at all times during simulation
· Recommendation card never below the fold
· C22 slider disabled when state = Danger or Conflict
· Budget = 0 → all sliders lock, warning fires
· Indicators (Confidence, Feasibility, Stress) always shown
Must Never Happen
· Two simultaneous verdicts or emotional messages
· "Apply Allocation" visible when state = Danger
· Numbers without context (just "68" with no reference)
· Positive framing when structural conflict is unresolvable
· Loading state on slider drag — must be instantaneous
· User reaches a dead end without a suggested next step
· Ranges presented as exact point estimates
